This comes up in nearly every quote conversation I have: *I can get it washed for twenty bucks — what am I paying you for?*

**Short version:** a car wash cleans the outside. A detail cleans the whole car — including the parts a wash physically cannot reach — and deals with what's set in rather than what's on top. Not two grades of the same service. Different problems.

## What a $20 tunnel wash actually does

It gets the loose dirt off the paint. On a normal week that's all most cars need. What it doesn't do:

- **Anything to the inside.** Not the carpets, seats, vents or the smell.
- **Tar, bug residue or sap.** These are bonded to the paint, not sitting on it. Sap just gets smeared into a wider, thinner film.
- **The wheel wells or the underside** — which, after a Massachusetts winter, is where the salt actually lives.
- **Door shuts, sills, fuel flap, boot channel.** Where grit collects.

There's also a cost that isn't on the sign. Most tunnels run a few hundred cars a day on the same brushes, so whatever the car before you brought in is what touches your paint. That's where the fine circular scratches on older cars come from.

Not a reason to never use one. A reason to know what you're buying.

## What a hand wash adds

A different process, not a slower one.

- **Two buckets.** One soap, one plain water for the mitt. The grit you pull off goes in the rinse bucket, not back onto the paint.
- **Wheels and wheel wells first, separate tools.** Brake dust is metallic. It never touches the paintwork.
- **Tar and bugs as their own step**, chemically, before anything is dragged across them.
- **Hand-dried** with clean towels, so no water spots — which matter more here than inland, because salt air marks as it dries.

Add a hand wax and there's a barrier on the clear coat for the season. Worth most on the water — [Beverly Farms](/mobile-detailing/beverly-farms) and [Manchester-by-the-Sea](/mobile-detailing/manchester-by-the-sea) get salt in the air year-round, not just when the roads are treated. That is all part of an [exterior hand wash](/exterior-hand-wash).

## What "detailing" actually means

On a full [interior detail](/interior-car-detailing):

- **Everything comes out.** Mats out, seats moved, boot emptied.
- **Vacuumed throughout**, including under the seats and along the rails, where most of the debris in any car hides.
- **Carpets and seats extracted.** Clean solution pushed into the pile and pulled straight back out. The step with no home equivalent — it lifts what's at the *backing* rather than what's on top. Beach sand, road salt, spilled coffee, whatever went in six months ago.
- **Every hard surface cleaned** — dash, console, door cards, cupholders, vents.
- **Glass inside and out.** The film inside a windscreen after a closed-up winter is what you notice at night.
- **Pet hair**, lifted out of the fabric before vacuuming.
- **Odour treated at the source**, then a light scent. Not the other way round.

In one line: **a wash removes what's on the surface. A detail removes what's gone into it.**

## What it costs around here

Honest ranges for the North Shore, based on what's publicly listed locally:

- **Tunnel wash:** $10–$25
- **Mobile exterior hand wash:** around $75 and up
- **Interior-only detail:** roughly $130–$230
- **Full interior and exterior:** roughly $190–$350

Two things before you compare quotes. **Pet hair is usually an add-on**, listed locally at $25–$75 on top — check whether your quote includes it. Mine does. And **ask what "starting at" means**: almost every published price is for a clean sedan, and a three-row SUV that's been to the beach all summer is not that car.

## Is it worth it?

Depends what's wrong with the car.

**A wash is right when** the inside is basically clean and you just want the road film off — or it's mid-winter and you're rinsing salt off between proper cleans. The January wash is worth more than the April one.

**A detail is worth it when:**

- **You're selling it or handing back a lease.** A clean interior changes what a buyer or inspector thinks the car has been through, for a fraction of what they'll knock off.
- **There's a smell.** Wet dog, spilled milk, old coffee, the musty edge from a damp coastal winter. Air fresheners don't fix it — it has to come out of the carpet and padding.
- **There's sand or salt in the carpet.** Neither comes out with a domestic vacuum, because neither is where suction reaches. That's [beach sand from June to September](/mobile-detailing/beverly) and road salt from December to March. Most cars here get both.
- **You have kids, a dog, or both.** You knew the answer already.
- **It's been a year or more.** You're resetting, not maintaining.

**Not worth it when** you had one two months ago and the car's been dry and empty since.

## What actually happens when you book one

Nobody explains this, and it's where most bad experiences come from:

**How long.** Three to five hours for a full interior; most of a session for both. Anyone promising a proper detail in forty-five minutes is doing a wash and calling it a detail.

**Do you need to be there?** No. Leave the keys somewhere sensible.

**What to take out first.** Anything you care about — cupholder change, sunglasses, garage remote, door-pocket paperwork, the kids' things off the seat backs. A cleared-out car gets a better job, because I can move the seats and get into the corners.

**Does it come back wet?** Damp, not wet. Properly extracted carpet dries in a few hours with the windows cracked. Soaked seats mean shampoo went in and didn't come back out.

**What I need on site.** An outdoor tap and a power outlet within reach of the car. Almost every driveway here has both. If you're on the street or in a shared lot — plenty of people are in [Salem](/mobile-detailing/salem) — tell me when you ask for the quote, not on the day.

**What it costs.** The price I quote is the price you pay, unless you add to the job. No deposit.

## How often should you detail your car?

Twice a year for most cars here:

- **March or April** — the post-winter reset. Salt out of the carpets, sills and wheel wells before it does lasting damage.
- **September or October** — beach sand, pollen, sap, bug splatter. Also the moment for a hand wax, before the salt starts.

Hand washes in between. If your car parks under trees in [Wenham](/mobile-detailing/wenham) or [Hamilton](/mobile-detailing/hamilton), sap season may set the schedule.
